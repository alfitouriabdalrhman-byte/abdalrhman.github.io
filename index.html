Reading Passage: 3D Printing

[1] 3D printing, also known as additive manufacturing, is a process of making three-dimensional solid objects from a digital file. This technology was first created in the 1980s, but it has become very popular only in the last few years. Today, it is used in many fields like medicine, engineering, and even fashion.
[2] To create an object, a designer first makes a 3D model on a computer using special software. Then, the 3D printer reads this file and starts building the object layer by layer. The printer continues to add these layers until the final product is finished. This process is very different from traditional manufacturing, where material is usually cut away.
[3] Different materials can be used in 3D printing. The most common material is plastic, because it is cheap and easy to use. However, modern printers can now use metal, glass, and even food like chocolate. Scientists are also experimenting with "bio-printing," using human cells to create artificial organs for hospitals.
[4] There are several advantages to this technology. First, it is very accurate and can create complex shapes that are impossible to make by hand. Second, it is faster for making prototypes (early models) of new products. Finally, 3D printers are becoming more portable and affordable, so some people now have them in their homes.
[5] However, there are some challenges. 3D printers can be slow when making large objects. Also, the materials used in printing can sometimes be weak compared to factory-made parts. Furthermore, there are concerns that people might use 3D printers to make dangerous items like weapons at home.
[6] In the future, experts believe that 3D printing will change the global economy. Instead of buying products from other countries and waiting for them to arrive by ship, people will just download a design and print it at home. This will save a lot of time and reduce the cost of transport.
